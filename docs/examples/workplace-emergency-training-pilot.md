# Workplace emergency preparedness: learning-challenge pilot

This is a **fictional, written design example**, not a deployed course, a real
emergency action plan, or a compliance determination. In an actual emergency,
follow the instructions of local authorities and your workplace's approved plan.
An employer and qualified safety reviewer must adapt and approve any real use.

## Inputs and boundaries

- **Audience:** employees at a fictional shared office; no prior knowledge assumed.
- **Objective:** given a fictional evacuation instruction, identify the next
  action described by that workplace's plan and where to report afterward.
- **Source context:** OSHA's [emergency preparedness overview](https://www.osha.gov/emergency-preparedness/getting-started)
  discusses planning, communication and evacuation; its [employee evacuation guidance](https://www.osha.gov/etools/evacuation-plans-procedures/eap/elements/employees)
  describes following the worksite plan and reporting to a designated meeting
  place. These US sources were inspected for this pilot on 2026-11-26. Their
  jurisdiction and applicability must be reassessed for a real organization.
- **Fictional plan for this exercise:** a drill announcement instructs employees
  to leave by the route marked in their local plan and report to their assigned
  assembly point. The supervisor is responsible for confirming attendance.
  These are exercise assumptions, not a statement about an actual workplace.
- **Missing for real use:** the current site-specific plan, accessible and
  alternative routes, local authority instructions, relevant jurisdiction,
  policy owner, review date, and approval by a qualified safety reviewer.

## One learning loop

**Decision.** During the fictional drill, the announcement says to follow the
workplace evacuation plan. Which action follows the fictional plan?

A. Leave using the route identified in the plan and report to the assigned assembly point.

B. Remain at the desk until a coworker returns with an update.

C. Go to an independently chosen location without telling the supervisor.

**Check answer:** A is supported by the exercise plan. B delays the stated
action; C omits the plan's reporting step. An actual response depends on the
specific situation, official instructions, and the approved local plan.

**Hint:** Find the two actions named in the fictional plan: where to go and
where to report. Allow a retry and retain the first response separately.

**Transfer.** A different fictional office uses a different designated route
and assembly point. An announcement directs an evacuation under its plan.
What source should an employee use to identify those two site-specific details?

**Expected reasoning:** use that office's current approved plan and applicable
official instructions; do not copy the first office's fictional locations.

## Review and acceptance

| Stage | Visible or actionable | Check before continuing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Fictional plan and source context | Clearly separate the real guidance from the made-up plan. |
| Decision | Three choices and a deliberate Check answer action | One answer follows the stated exercise assumptions. |
| Feedback | Selected answer, expected answer and explanation | Explain why each alternative misses a stated step; allow retry. |
| Transfer | New office with different details | Learner identifies the new office's plan without relying on memorized locations. |

This document demonstrates the workflow only. No learner study, software
interaction, accessibility test, safety approval, or training effectiveness
has been established. A real implementation would need source review, policy
owner approval, accessible presentation and an independent transfer check.
