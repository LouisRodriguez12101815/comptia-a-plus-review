Distance-vector routers pass routes along

A distance-vector router learns a route from a neighbor and re-advertises it. It does not check the route or keep a topology map, which is why the process is compared to gossip.

AThey track the status of routes learned. They do not check route status.
BThey re-advertise routes learned. Each router passes along what it heard.
CEach router keeps its own topology database. That is a link-state trait.
DEach router checks the routes it learns. They do not verify them.

Question 20 answer is B. The re-advertising is what makes distance-vector routing slow to catch problems and prone to loops.
