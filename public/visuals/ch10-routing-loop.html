What a routing loop looks like

A routing loop sends a packet around a circle of routers. It never reaches the destination. Each hop lowers the TTL, and the packet is dropped when the TTL hits 0.

APackets go out one interface and come back on a different one.
BPackets move between a series of routers and never reach the destination. This is the loop.
CThe TTL expires before reaching the network. This is a result of the loop, not its definition.
DPackets take an inefficient path.

Question 12 answer is B. C describes what happens after a loop starts, but B describes the loop itself.
